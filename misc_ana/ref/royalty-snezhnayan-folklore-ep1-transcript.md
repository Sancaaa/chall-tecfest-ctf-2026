The Tsaritsa's full name is Anastasya Feodorovna Naya, and it's the Feodorovna part that's interesting here, because it's not what I would call a middle name, even though it's kind of presented as one. See, in Slavic nations, it's quite common for people to have a first or given name, a surname or family name, and then a patronymic name, which as far as I can tell is often used as a replacement or an amendment to a middle name. Now, a patronymic name is a type of name that references the given name of the individual's father or other notable male ancestor. So, if you had two men who were born to a family line, their children would share the same surname in reference to their shared family line, but both children would have different patronymic names because they would both have different fathers. Make sense? Good!

Because Tsaritsa's patronymic name is Feodorovna, which means her father's name was Theodor or Feodor, which is decidedly not Monomakh's name in any capacity. Basically, any woman who married into the Romanovs often adopted the patronymic name Feodorovna. In fact, this was the case with Anastasia's mother, Alexandra. But it gets more interesting than this, because the reason Feodorovna was chosen as the patronymic name has to do with the *Our Lady of St. Theodore* icon, or the *Feodorovskaya* icon of the Mother of God—so basically a specific depiction of the Virgin Mary and her son. This icon was venerated by the Romanov family to such a degree that the women marrying into the family adopted the name in honor of it. Now, this much I know for sure, like it's a fact. But there is another theory that posits the idea that Alexander Yaroslavich Nevsky, the 12th century prince of Novgorod, was the one who originally commissioned the painting of the Virgin Mary and her son, and his Christian name was Theodore, so it was basically called his icon, Theodore's icon. Regardless of which theory is actually the correct one, the end result is the same.
